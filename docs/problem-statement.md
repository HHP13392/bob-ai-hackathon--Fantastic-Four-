# Problem Statement
Utility companies struggle to identify and respond to high-risk grid assets before equipment failure occurs because sensor data, weather information, asset health records, and maintenance operations are fragmented across different systems. As a result, control-room operators spend valuable time analyzing information manually, critical risks may be overlooked, maintenance becomes reactive, crews are deployed late, and the probability of equipment failure, service disruption, and safety incidents increases.

## Background

Electric utility companies manage many important assets, such as substations, transformers, transmission equipment, and other grid infrastructure.

## The Problem

Utility control-room operators and maintenance coordinators struggle to turn fragmented sensor, weather, and asset information into fast, reliable maintenance decisions. Existing systems often show individual readings or alerts but do not clearly rank asset risk, combine environmental conditions, or connect warnings to a specific response plan. This can lead to delayed inspections, reactive maintenance, inefficient crew deployment, higher operational costs, and increased risk of equipment failure or power interruption.

## Who is Affected

Primary User: Utility Control-Room Operators
The main users affected are utility control-room operators and grid reliability coordinators who monitor the condition of substations, transformers, transmission equipment, and other critical grid assets.
